The LP finds the minimum staff needed to legally operate under standard child-to-staff ratios (1:3 infants, 1:5 toddlers, 1:8 pre-school, 1:12 school-age). This is your cost floor — you cannot legally go below it, regardless of budget.

A second, separate model: instead of allocating staff to roles, this allocates the entire monthly operating budget across every cost category to find the minimum-cost way to legally and competitively run the centre at a target enrolment.

[SVG]
How this model is built
  • Every category floor below is its own slider
  • Staff cost floor is the one exception: it's computed live from Tab 1's ratio-optimal staffing plan, recomputed for whatever enrolment n you set below — model composition, not a guess.
  • Because every constraint here is a lower bound and every cost coefficient is positive, the mathematically optimal (minimum-cost) solution is always to fund each category at exactly its floor — the model's job is to add them up correctly and check the total fits your budget.
  • Fixed costs (rent, utilities, insurance, marketing, activities) don't change with enrolment — they're a flat monthly range. Variable costs (food, supplies, materials, maintenance) are priced per child and scale with n.

Break-even is a linear equation in one variable: the enrolment n where total revenue equals total cost. Cost is split into fixed items (don't change with enrolment) and variable items (scale per child) — build both from your surveyed ranges.

[SVG]
Variables to determine from Sections D & E of your Data Collection Form
  • Q9 — monthly fee per child (bins: <5k / 5–10k / 10–15k / >15k) → sets the fee slider.
  • Q10 — whether additional fees (registration, meals, transport) are charged → toggle below adds them to revenue.
  • Q11 Rent, Q12 Utilities, Q14 Staff salary, Q15 Other (maintenance, insurance, misc.) → sum of researched mid-points sets Fixed Cost.
  • Q16 Annual activities (play group, swimming, concerts, workshops, field trips) → amortised into Fixed Cost below.
  • Q18 Marketing budget, split by the channel mix from Q17 → added to Fixed Cost.
  • Q13 Food & supplies plus activity/maintenance spend, divided by the enrolment you surveyed it at → sets the Variable-Cost-per-child breakdown.

The logistic growth model applies when growth is constrained by capacity. It gives an S-shaped curve — fast early growth that plateaus near capacity K. The break-even line is pulled live from Tab 2, so it updates if you change the fee or cost breakdown.

[SVG]

  • Q3, Q6 — years operating and current enrolment → informs a realistic starting point N₀.
  • Q5 — maximum capacity → sets K.
  • Growth rate r is not collected by the form directly; estimate it from how quickly comparable centres filled up (ask in follow-up interviews or use the slider to test scenarios). Kept fully adjustable, as before.

Net profit = Revenue − Operating cost − Depreciation. Operating cost and fee are pulled live from Tab 2. Depreciation spreads each capital asset's cost over its useful life (straight-line) — edit the cost and life of any asset below to match your own quotations.

[SVG]
Variables to determine
  • Fee, Fixed Cost and Variable-Cost/child are inherited from Tab 2 — set them there.
  • Capital expenditure items — replace the cost of each asset with your actual supplier quotations.
  • Useful life (years) — replace with the depreciation policy you intend to use (equipment, furniture, IT etc. typically 3–10 years); this determines annual/monthly depreciation.
  • One-off items (legal & licensing, launch marketing, working-capital reserve) are capital outlay but are not depreciated — they are shown separately.
EBITDA(n) = Revenue(n) − OperatingCost(n) = fee·n − (FC + VC·n)
Depreciation/mo = Σ (AssetCost_i / UsefulLife_i) / 12
Net profit(n) = EBITDA(n) − Depreciation/mo
